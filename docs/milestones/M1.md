### Live check to do (user, macOS and Windows)

You need a Twitch app first. Register it at dev.twitch.tv/console under StreamGremlinHQ (D-005):
- give it any unique name;
- set Client Type to **Public** (a desktop app can't keep a secret);
- set the OAuth Redirect URL to `https://localhost`. Twitch refuses `http://` ("Redirect URIs must use HTTPS protocol."), and sign-in never uses it;
- copy the **Client ID**.

Please note what the console asked for, so the ADR can record it.

1. Build: `make desktop-ui desktop build`, then open `./bin/app/StreamGremlin`. Press Ctrl/⌘+2 for **Connections**.
2. Paste the Client ID and save. "Your account" offers **Sign in with Twitch**.
3. Sign in with your own account. Your browser should open twitch.tv/activate. Enter the code shown in the app and authorize. Within a few seconds the app should say "Signed in as <you>".
4. **Bot account:** click Sign in with Twitch, then open the activate link in a **private window** and sign in as StreamGremlinBot. The app should say "Signed in as streamgremlinbot". Then try signing the bot in with your own account: it should be refused, saying it's your own account.
5. Quit and reopen the app. Both accounts should still be signed in. They show "Unknown" for a moment while checking.
6. `./bin/streamgremlin twitch` shows the same.
7. Sign the bot out (the app asks first). On twitch.tv, as StreamGremlinBot, Settings → Connections should no longer list the app (it may take a minute).
8. On Windows: steps 1 to 6. The secrets go to Credential Manager.

To try it without registering an app first, see "mocktwitch" in CONTRIBUTING.md.

**Results:**
- **App registration, 2026-11-08, the user:** the console refused an `http://` redirect URL ("Redirect URIs must use HTTPS protocol."). It was registered as a Public client with `https://localhost`, and the docs were corrected.
- **Steps 1 to 4 on macOS, 2026-11-08, the user:** the Client ID was pasted, and both accounts signed in against the real Twitch ("both accounts show as signed in"). This is the first sign-in against the real Twitch.

**Platform capability notes:** `chat.read`, `chat.send` and `chat.bot_moderator` in the manifest. In short:

- The Chat Bot badge needs an app token, which a public app can't have.
