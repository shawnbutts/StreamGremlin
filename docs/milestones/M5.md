## Slice 1: what plugins and themes own

**Written** ([ADR-0036](../adr/0036-packages-plugins-and-themes.md), D-063), before any plugin or theme code (LESSONS-LEARNED item 11):
- one package format for both kinds: a ZIP with `streamgremlin.json`, read with limits and stored in the database, so backups carry it and what runs is what was checked;
- an id and version are one fixed thing; the publisher is shown as stated, not verified;
- installing asks first and changes nothing until used; another publisher's id needs confirming by name;
- what each kind owns: files, settings, actions, events, offered workflows, commands, points rounds, audit;
- updates keep valid settings and ask again for more; the two previous versions are kept for Roll back;
- removing a theme returns its overlays to Classic; removing a plugin turns off, never deletes, the workflows that use it;
- themes run on a second loopback origin in a sandboxed frame with no network, talk only through the checked message protocol, and never decide or time a result; a broken theme falls back to Classic for that spin;
- installed themes are labelled as not checked for odds; built-in looks draw wedges to their real size.

**Checked:** the design against StreamWisp's ADR-0041, ADR-0067, D-094, D-106, D-107 and D-111, the theme concept's protocol and packaging notes, the overlay server (ADR-0017, ADR-0030), backups (ADR-0012) and obs-browser's README for `window.obsstudio`.

**To check in slice 2:** OBS's browser source with the sandboxed frame and its policy, on macOS and Windows, and whether `window.obsstudio` reaches a sandboxed frame.

## Slice 2: built-in wheel looks

**Built** ([ADR-0036](../adr/0036-packages-plugins-and-themes.md), D-064):
- a wheel overlay's **Look**: Classic (drawn by the overlay page, as before), Workshop, Neon arcade or Selector board, each with a title (empty: the wheel's own), whether to show it, and an accent colour;
- the **theme server**: a second loopback origin, at any free port, serving only the built-in looks' files, each with a policy that sandboxes it (`sandbox allow-scripts`), allows no network, no frames, workers or forms, and lets only overlay pages and the app frame it;
- the overlay page frames the look sandboxed (`allow-scripts` only, no referrer), plays spins one after another on the engine's clock, plays the sounds, and tells the look what to show; the look can only say it's ready, that an animation finished, or that it couldn't show a spin, checked for source, size (16 KiB), kind and rate (20 a second);
- **Classic as the fallback:** a spin before the look is ready shows in Classic; a look that reports an error for the spin on screen is hidden, and Classic shows that spin stopped at once;
- the looks are StreamGremlin's own code, drawn from the concept's designs: wedges sized to their weights and stopping on the drawn number (Workshop, Neon arcade), or each choice with its chance (Selector board); labels are text only;
- the page says when the look is ready and whether OBS's page API reached it: in the app's preview, and in OBS with `?status=1`; the engine logs it;
- `overlays look NAME -look workshop|neon-arcade|selector-board|classic -look-title T -look-show-title=false -accent #RRGGBB`;
- `GET /v1/overlays` lists the looks with their settings; `PUT` takes `look.theme` and `look.theme_settings` (another look's settings replace them; a change that doesn't mention them keeps them).

**Checked:**
- unit tests: looks and settings checked (unknown looks and settings, types, lengths, control characters, colours), only wheel overlays have one, defaults fill gaps, the hello names the look's page;
- the theme server: its policy on every file, every file a look loads is served and none come from elsewhere, nothing else is served (another build's, unknown looks, the overlay page's files, traversal, other types, POST, another host name);
- the overlay page: the sandboxed frame, a spin on the engine's clock with its sounds, the queue, Classic for a look not ready or failing, what a look may not send, settings in place, back to Classic, and a link anywhere but the theme server never framed;
- the looks: ready with whether OBS's page API reached them, wedges at their real size, stopping on the drawn number, labels as text, a spin that doesn't add up refused, messages from anyone but the framing page ignored, reduced motion, the board's chances;
- the Overlays screen: picking a look, its settings, the odds and Page permissions notes, back to Classic, axe;
- the API, and an acceptance test through the real engine and CLI;
- `make verify` and `make windows`.

**Fixed while testing:** switching back to Classic was ignored, because an empty look was left out of the request; it's now always sent.

## Slice 3b, part 1: add-on looks and OBS's Page permissions (D-067)

Slice 3a's live check showed OBS's page API reaches a look's sandboxed frame. A look could use whatever the browser source's **Page permissions** allow, up to ending the stream.

**Built:**
- the looks report the level OBS gives their frame (`getControlLevel`); with `?status=1` the status line names it, like "OBS page API reachable, at level 1 (Read access to OBS status information)";
- the overlay page asks OBS its own level and frames an add-on look only at level 0, "No access to OBS" (or outside OBS). At a higher level, an unknown one, or while waiting, Classic shows, without the look's sounds; why is said once in the status line and the engine's log. Built-in looks load at any level;
- the hello marks an installed look `add_on`; a link off the built-in path counts as an add-on anyway;
- the Overlays, Add-ons and `packages install` wording.

**Checked:**
- the overlay page: an add-on look outside OBS and at level 0 loads; at level 4 Classic shows with no look sounds, said once; a built-in look at level 4 loads; a link off the built-in path counts as an add-on; waiting for OBS's answer, no answer in 2 seconds, an OBS that can't say;
- the looks: the level they report, or none if OBS doesn't answer in a second; the status line naming it;
- the hello's `add_on`; the Overlays and Add-ons wording; `packages install`'s wording in the acceptance test;
- `make verify` and `make windows`.

### Live check (user, macOS or Windows; OBS)

1. Build: `make verify`, and start the app. Install Paper wheel if it isn't, and pick it for your wheel overlay.
2. In OBS, add `?status=1` to the wheel source's URL. With **Page permissions** at "No access to OBS", spin: Paper wheel shows, and the status line says "Paper wheel ready; OBS page API reachable; OBS didn’t tell the look its level" (Paper wheel doesn't ask; the page did, and let it load).
3. Raise **Page permissions** to "Read access to OBS status information" and refresh the source. Spin: Classic shows, and the status line says why ("Paper wheel not loaded: … Page permissions are “Read access to OBS status information”…").
4. Switch the overlay's look to **Workshop** and spin: Workshop shows, and the status line says "… at level 1 (Read access to OBS status information)". That confirms the frame gets the source's level.
5. Set Page permissions back to "No access to OBS", remove `?status=1`, and refresh: Paper wheel shows again.

**First try (2026-11-11):** at "Read access to OBS status information", Workshop's status line said OBS page API reachable at an unknown level: OBS never answered the look's `getControlLevel`. OBS's source ([obs-browser](https://github.com/obsproject/obs-browser), `browser-app.cpp`) explains it: `window.obsstudio` is put in every frame's context, a call is sent to OBS with no note of which frame made it, OBS checks it against the **source's** Page permissions, and the answer is run in the **main frame**. By that code, the look's calls reach OBS with the source's level and only the answers don't come back (read from the source, not tried). Answers don't matter for "switch scene" or "stop streaming", so D-067 stands. The status line now says "OBS didn't tell the look its level" for this.

**Result (2026-11-11):** passed. At "Read access to OBS status information", Paper wheel wasn't loaded. The user: "step 3, yes. classic showed and the status showed the "not loaded" message." At "No access to OBS" it loaded. The user: "with "no access to OBS" the paper wheel version showed". Step 4 is the first try above: OBS doesn't tell a look its level.

## Slice 4: Lua scripts (D-069)

**Built** ([ADR-0037](../adr/0037-lua-scripts.md)):
- **"Run a Lua script"** (`lua.run`, under More steps): the script written in the step, literal (`${…}` refused on save) and at most 64 KiB; **Values to pass** (`sg.args`); a time limit of 0.1 to 30 seconds (1 unless set);
- in a script: `sg.args`, `sg.event`, `sg.log(...)` and `print(...)`, and `base`, `table`, `string`, `math` and `os.time`/`os.date`/`os.clock`; no `io`, `debug`, `package`, `require`, `load*`, `coroutine` or the rest of `os`; a fresh state each run; `math.random` from `crypto/rand`, `math.randomseed` doing nothing; Lua 5.1's `string.format`, widths at most 99; `string.rep` at most 1 MiB;
- the step's output: `result` (a value, list or table), `log` (up to 100 lines) and `log_dropped`; a result at most 32 deep, 10,000 values and 64 KiB; whole numbers stay whole;
- **`internal/lua`:** the worker is the app's own binary started as `__lua-worker`, with an empty environment, in the temporary folder, talking in length-prefixed JSON frames (1 MiB at most) and checking the script's digest; the engine kills it 250 ms past its time limit or when its run is cancelled; its heap is capped at 64 MiB (a 5 ms watchdog, exit code 3); at most 4 at once and 2 idle; a worker that timed out by itself is kept, one killed or crashed never is;
- every run logged with its workflow, run, step, script digest, time taken and how it ended; every log line; a killed worker and why; a crashed worker's last words;
- **Try it** in the step editor: the result or error, the log, and a note when values from the event were sent as written; `POST /v1/lua/try` (`workflows.write`); `streamgremlin lua try FILE [-arg NAME=VALUE] [-time-limit S] [-json]`;
- parameters can carry `max_length`, checked on save; Help has a Lua scripts entry.

**Checked:**
- the runtime: values in and out, whole numbers, mixed tables, logs (100 lines, long lines cut, lines before a failure kept), error messages ("line 2: …", "line 2, column 6 near 'return': syntax error"), everything removed from the sandbox, a fresh state each run, `math.random`'s ranges and seeding doing nothing, `string.format` (each conversion, widths over 99, unknown verbs), `string.rep` over 1 MiB, what can't be returned (functions, NaN, a table inside itself, too many values, too big, odd keys), the time limit inside the worker (kept for reuse), the step's own deadline winning, a hung worker killed at the limit and grace, the memory cap, cancellation, a worker that crashes (its stderr logged), the worker's environment (none of the engine's), 12 runs at once, after Close, the digest check, frames too big or not JSON; with the race detector;
- the step: the result and log, a test event running it, errors with the last log line, time limits out of range or not numbers, values that aren't names and values, no runner; saving refuses a computed script and one over 64 KiB;
- the API: a result, a given event, failures as answers (script, timeout, output), bad requests, no token;
- the step editor: typing, Try it with the step's values and time limit, values from the event sent as written, a failure with its log, a computed script refused, the size near the limit, axe;
- acceptance through the real engine and CLI: `lua try` with a value, a loop stopped at 0.3 s, a memory bomb ending its worker and not the engine, the sandbox, a workflow running it for a test event with the next step reading its result, a computed script refused on save, the engine's log, no workers left after the engine is killed;
- the desktop binary as a worker: scripts run in it (about 0.4 s for the first, then about 0.1 ms each), with no window and no process left behind;
- `make verify` and `make windows`.
