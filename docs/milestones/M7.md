M7 is done when the user has used it on a real stream on macOS and Windows, and the result is written below.

Each slice checks Twitch's and Elgato's current docs first, and records the scopes, limits and anything uncertain in the capability manifest. Nothing outside their documented APIs is built.

## How it's meant to work

- **One place while live.** Chat fills the middle. The buttons the streamer made are beside it or above it, and pressing one runs the workflows it starts. Nothing needs another screen during a stream.
- **Buttons are a trigger.** "You press a button" names the button, so a Live screen button, a Stream Deck key and a workflow test all start the same workflows. A button has no viewer, so a step that needs one is refused, as with timers.
- **Unknown stays unknown.** A viewer card shows only what Twitch says or StreamGremlin recorded. A missing scope or a failed call says so, and doesn't guess.
- **Moderating is Twitch's.** StreamGremlin calls Twitch's documented endpoints as the streamer, or as the bot when it's a moderator and the streamer chooses that. It records each one, and says when Twitch refuses one and why.
- **The Stream Deck is another client.** Like the app and CLI, it reaches the engine through the authenticated local API. Its token is narrow: it can list and press buttons, run workflows and read their state, and nothing else. It's paired once on purpose, and can be removed.

### Live check for slice 1 (user, macOS and Windows)

1. Build: `make verify`, and start the app. **Live** is second in the sidebar (Ctrl/⌘+2).
2. With Twitch signed in, chat in your channel from another account: the message shows on Live as it arrives, with the name, badges as one letter (the word on hover; changed after the slice 3 live check) and a colour swatch. Scroll up, chat again: a "1 new message ↓" button appears; it takes you back down.
3. Send a message from the box as you, then (if a bot is signed in) as the bot. Both show in Twitch chat and on Live.
4. **Edit buttons** → **New button**: "Be right back", blue. Save: it says no workflow starts from it yet. **Make a workflow**: the editor opens with "You press a button" and Be right back picked. Write a message, save, and turn the workflow on (Workflows).
5. **Done**, then press **Be right back**: chat gets the message, and Live says "Pressed “Be right back”: started …". Press it a few times quickly: each press is one message.
6. Make a second button, move it before the first, then switch **Buttons: Top** and back to **Right**. Restart the app: the choice is kept.
7. Delete the second button: the dialog says it's deleted for good. Delete Be right back: the dialog names its workflow as kept.
8. In a terminal: `streamgremlin buttons`, and `streamgremlin buttons press ID` with a button's id: its workflow runs.

**Result (2026-11-11):** passed on macOS and Windows. The user: "live check looks good. on thing that this pointed out, when creating a workflow (manually or from an addon), it should be enabled." That's D-077, below.

## New workflows start on (D-077)

**Built:** the engine saves a new workflow on, unless a chat command it answers is taken by a workflow that's on, and then saves it off and says why (`off_because` on `PUT /v1/workflows/{id}`). Saving keeps an existing workflow's switch. The editor, Timers and Live say "It's on." or why not after saving; so does `streamgremlin workflows save`. Help says so.

**Checked:** the engine (new on; new with a taken command off, with why; changes keeping the switch either way; restore still off); the API; the screens' words; acceptance through the CLI; `make verify` and `make windows`.

### Live check (user)

1. Workflows → **New workflow** (or a template from the gallery, or Dice tray's): save it. It says "It's on.", and its switch is on.
2. Make another workflow answering the same `!command` as one that's on: it says "It's off: !… is already answered by …", and its switch is off.
3. On Live, **Make a workflow** for a button and save it: press the button, and the workflow runs without turning it on first.

### Live check for slice 2 (user, macOS and Windows)

1. Build and start the app. **Connections** asks you to sign in to Twitch again, for two new permissions (moderators and bans, and VIPs). Sign in. Until you do, the card shows those facts as unknown, saying to sign in again.
2. On **Live**, have someone chat (or chat from a second account). Rest the pointer on their name: the card opens beside it, with when the account was made, following since, subscribed (tier, gift), VIP, moderator and banned. Move away: it closes.
3. Check the card against Twitch's own viewer card for the same person: follow date, sub tier, VIP and moderator agree.
4. Make the second account a VIP on Twitch (`/vip name`), then **Refresh** on the card: VIP says Yes. Time them out for a minute on Twitch (`/timeout name 60`): **Refresh** shows "Timed out until …". After the minute, **Refresh** shows No.
5. Tab to a name and press Enter: the card opens with focus in it. Escape closes it and focus is back on the name.
6. In a terminal: `streamgremlin viewers card @name` prints the same facts and their recent messages.

### Live check for slice 3 (user, macOS and Windows)

You'll need a second Twitch account in your chat. Do this off stream or with a friendly viewer.

1. Start the app. **Connections** asks you to sign in to Twitch again, for the moderating permissions. Sign in.
2. On **Live**, have the second account chat. Click the gear (⚙) beside its name: **Delete message…**, **Time out 10 minutes…**, **Ban…** and **More in their card…**. **Delete message…** asks first; Cancel, check nothing happened, then delete: it's removed from Twitch chat, and the line shows Deleted.
3. **Time out 10 minutes…** (asks first): Twitch chat shows the timeout, and Live says "Timed out … for 10 minutes." Click their name: Banned says "Timed out until …". **Unban** in the card lifts it.
4. In the card: type a reason, **Warn…** (asks first). The second account sees Twitch's warning to acknowledge. **Make VIP**, then **Remove VIP**; **Make moderator…** (asks first), then **Remove moderator**. Each matches what Twitch shows.
5. **Ban…** asks first. Cancel, then ban for real, then **Unban**.
6. **Shoutout…** (asks first) for an account that streams (Twitch needs you live for this one; off stream it says Twitch refused, in Twitch's words).
7. **Chat modes**: turn on **Slow** (30 seconds), **Emote-only** and **Followers-only**, and check each in Twitch chat; turn them off.
8. **Restrict** the second account from its card: its messages now reach only you and your mods, and Live marks them "Suspicious: … restricted". **Treat as usual** ends it.
9. **Done from StreamGremlin** lists everything above. In a terminal, `streamgremlin mod history` shows the same.
10. If you see a real suspicious-user flag from Twitch while live (a likely or possible ban evader), note what Live showed.
11. **After the live check (D-076, D-079 changes):** the gear (⚙) menu and its questions; badges as letters. **Edit buttons** → Edit **Be right back** → search "pause", pick **player-pause**, tick **Show only the icon**, Save: the button shows only the icon, and hovering it names it. Make two more icon-only buttons, one from **Nerd Fonts** (search "microphone off"): the three sit side by side as small squares, right and top, and the Nerd Fonts icon draws (not a box). Check the Buttons and Chat modes panels have room at the edges and look compact, right and top.
