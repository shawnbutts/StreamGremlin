Everything it does

StreamGremlin is one app for running a Twitch stream, with YouTube alongside. Every feature has a screen, and Help explains each one. Here’s the whole list.

Workflows

When this happens, do that

  • Triggers: chat commands and messages, a viewer’s first words this stream, follows, subs, resubs, gifts, cheers, raids, channel point redemptions, Hype Trains, polls and predictions, ad breaks, going live and offline, OBS scene changes, timers, countdowns, your buttons, and add-on events.
  • Steps: send chat, play a sound, show an alert, switch scenes, show or hide sources, give or take points, spin a wheel, run a poll, change the title or category, make a clip, add a stream marker, moderate, post to Discord, save variables, run another workflow, and run Lua.
  • Templates for the usual jobs, in a gallery: pick one, change anything, save.
  • Test runs a workflow without reaching chat or OBS. Every real run has a trace: what each step was given and gave back, or why it didn’t run.
  • One answer per command, groups with their own switch, variables kept between streams, and deleted workflows you can restore.

Live

Run your stream from one screen

  • Twitch and YouTube chat together, each line marked, with a box to send as you or your bot.
  • Your own buttons, with colours and icons, on the right or along the top. A button runs the workflows that start from it.
  • A viewer card on every name: following, subscribed, VIP, moderator, banned, their points and recent messages. Anything Twitch didn’t say shows as unknown.
  • Moderate from chat: delete, time out, ban, unban, warn, shoutout, VIP and moderator, chat modes and Shield Mode. Anything that can’t be undone asks first.

Overlays and sounds

Alerts in OBS

  • Each overlay is one OBS browser source with a private link. Alerts come one at a time, with the viewer’s profile picture and a sound if you like.
  • Now playing shows the song your music player writes out, with its album art.
  • Clock and timers: the time, a “Starting soon” countdown, or a stopwatch, started from a button or a workflow.
  • Wheels and giveaways have their own overlays. Wheels come in several looks, and add-on looks can be installed.
  • A sound library with a volume for each sound.

Giveaways and wheels

Fair draws, on the record

  • Viewers enter with a command, once each. Limit entry to followers, older accounts, subscribers or regular chatters, and give subscribers extra chances (chat is told).
  • Or draw at once from everyone who chatted recently.
  • Winners are drawn on your computer with a cryptographically secure random source, and every entry list and draw is recorded, so Check the draws can confirm them.
  • Wheels show each wedge at its real size, so the odds you see are the real odds.
  • A practice giveaway uses test chat and sends nothing.

Viewers and points

Your channel’s own points

  • Name your points. Viewers earn by chatting, time in chat, follows, subs, gifted subs, cheers, and on YouTube by memberships and Super Chats.
  • Give to everyone in chat, every subscriber, or everyone who chatted recently, and take it back if it was a mistake.
  • An append-only ledger: nothing is changed or deleted, nobody goes below zero, and a retried run never pays twice. Check the ledger proves it.
  • A record for every viewer, with export and “forget me”. Twitch and YouTube accounts can be linked to share one balance, by you or by the viewer with !link.

Protection

Chat filters, raids and bots

  • Chat filters for links (with allowed sites and !permit), capitals, symbols, emotes, repeats, your own words, brand-new accounts and text art. Each deletes, times out or warns, and never touches you, your mods or your bot.
  • Twitch’s Blocked Terms, listed and copied either way.
  • A raid guard that spots a burst of new accounts, and a one-press Lock chat.
  • Ban lists from a file, and a follow-bot review: more below.

Ban lists

Ban a shared list of thousands, safely

When a community shares a list of hate-raid or spam accounts, import it and StreamGremlin works through it in the background.

  • Any size you’re likely to meet: a text file of Twitch names or user ids, or a CSV with a login or id column, up to 5 million lines.
  • Checked before anything happens. Every name is looked up on Twitch first, then you see how many it will ban, how many are banned already, which don’t exist, and who it leaves alone: you, your mods and VIPs, and viewers with points. Nothing is banned until you press Start banning.
  • A progress bar for every step: reading the file, looking names up, banning, undoing and copying, each with its pace and about when it will finish.
  • At Twitch’s pace, always keeping room for chat and your own moderating, and waiting while you’re live if you like. Pause and resume any time; after a restart it carries on where it left off, checking any ban whose answer never came before sending it again.
  • Stay in sync. Import a newer version under the same name and only the new names are checked and banned. Names gone from it can be unbanned, but only bans the list made. Someone you or a mod unbanned by hand is noticed and left alone.
  • Undo lifts exactly what the list banned and nothing else. Copy Twitch’s ban list and Save to a file keep a copy of your own.
  • Follow-bot review: finds follows from accounts made days before that never chatted, marks the ones that came in a burst, and blocks or bans the ones you pick, as a list you can undo.

And also

The rest of the workshop

  • Timers

    Every so often or on a schedule, only while you’re live, only when chat is active, or once.

  • Quotes

    Numbered quotes viewers see with !quote and mods add with !quoteadd. Deleting one never renumbers the rest.

  • Channel commands

    Templates for !title, !game, !uptime, !followage, !clip and !marker.

  • Rewards and polls

    Make and manage channel point rewards, mark redemptions done or refund them, and run polls and predictions. Affiliates and Partners.

  • Ads

    See your next ad and snoozes, run or snooze one, and tell chat when a break starts.

  • OBS

    Scenes, sources, filters, mute, media, recording and replay. Switch for now and come back, without undoing a scene you chose yourself.

  • YouTube

    Live chat beside Twitch’s, Super Chats, memberships, polls and moderation, with the quota shown as you go.

  • Stream Deck

    An official plugin: pair it with a code, and keys press your buttons. A paired deck can do nothing else.

  • Discord

    Workflows post to your server through webhooks: going live, new clips, and a summary when you go offline.

  • Lua and add-ons

    Lua steps for anything else, and plugins that add steps and triggers. Each runs in its own process, with only the permissions you allow.

  • Backups

    Everything copied every day and whenever you ask. Restoring asks first and backs up what it replaces.

  • Made comfortable

    Dark, light and midnight themes, four text sizes, keyboard shortcuts, screens you don’t use tucked under More, and accessibility checks in every theme.

  • A command line

    streamgremlin does what the app does, from a terminal, through the same local API.

Not yet

What isn’t here

So you know before you switch:

  • Importing from Streamer.bot comes after the first public release.
  • Signed installers: the beta isn’t signed yet, so Windows and macOS warn the first time. The download page says what to do.
  • Kick isn’t planned.
  • StreamGremlin runs while your computer does: it isn’t a cloud bot.
