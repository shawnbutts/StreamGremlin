Privacy

The short version: StreamGremlin runs on your computer, keeps what it knows there, and has no servers of its own to send anything to.

The app

What it keeps, and where

StreamGremlin keeps your workflows, settings, events, chat it has seen, viewers’ records and points, and its logs in one data folder on your computer. Sign-ins and passwords (Twitch, YouTube, OBS, Discord webhooks) go in your system’s keyring: Keychain on macOS, Credential Manager on Windows, or Secret Service on Linux, or an encrypted file in the data folder where none answers. They never appear in logs, backups or exports.

What it sends, and to whom

Only to the services you connect, and only to do what you set up:

  • Twitch, to read and send chat, hear your channel’s events, and moderate, as you or your bot account.
  • YouTube, if you sign in, to read and send live chat and the like, through a Google project of your own.
  • OBS, on your own computer or network, to switch scenes and show sources.
  • Discord, if you add a webhook, to post what your workflows say.
  • GitHub, to check for a newer StreamGremlin when it starts and once a day. It asks for the list of StreamGremlin’s releases and says only that it’s StreamGremlin and its version. GitHub sees your computer’s internet address, as any website does. You can turn this off in Settings, under Updates. Updates are downloaded from GitHub only when you choose to install one.

There is no StreamGremlin account, no analytics and no crash reporting. StreamGremlin never sends viewers’ names to anyone else, such as bot-list services.

Your viewers

StreamGremlin records the viewers it sees in your chat and events, so points, giveaways and moderation work. On the Viewers screen you can export one viewer’s record, or forget a viewer completely if they ask.

Reports you send

If you send a bug report with your logs, the logs contain chat names and messages from your stream, though never passwords or sign-ins. Send them only to the person you’re reporting to.

This website

This site is plain pages. It sets no cookies and runs no scripts, analytics or trackers, and it loads nothing from other sites. The server that hosts it may keep ordinary access logs (such as your IP address and the page asked for), as web servers do.

Questions

Ask on GitHub. For security problems, see Security.
